# [Modulation Identification] IQFormer: A Novel Multimodal Fusion Modulation Identification Model Based on Transformers [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: The continuous growth in the number of wireless communication devices has placed increasing pressure on modern systems' communication monitoring and spectrum management. There is a pressing need for technologies that can improve spectral utilization and throughput. Automated Modulation Recognition (AMR) intelligently identifies the modulation mode of received signals under unknown channels and prior conditions. Its goal is to provide prior information for signal demodulation and subsequent analysis, enhancing system cognitive capabilities and improving spectral throughput efficiency [1]. AMR has wide applications in both civilian and military fields, especially in non-cooperative communication scenarios such as dynamic spectrum access, interference detection, and military reconnaissance. Effectively extracting distinctive and robust modulation features is crucial for the development of AMR technology.
Traditional AMR methods can be divided into likelihood-based AMR (LB-AMR) methods [3], [4] and feature-based AMR (FB-AMR) methods [5], [6]. LB-AMR methods use the likelihood ratio function at high complexity to trade off strong classification performance but are no longer suitable for modern wireless communication systems with real-time requirements. FB-AMR methods rely on expert knowledge for manual feature selection and combine simple classifiers to train supervised learning models. However, FB-AMR methods often overly depend on expert prior knowledge, which may lead to suboptimal solutions and their scalability is not sufficient to meet the growing demand for new modulation pattern recognition.
Deep Learning-based AMR (DL-AMR) has become a new solution in recent years [8]. Due to the superior performance demonstrated by Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) [9]-[11] on complex value time-radio signals, several DL-AMR methods have been developed using raw signals as inputs paired with CNN or Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) [12]-[14]. However, CNNs lack temporal sensitivity and suffer from localized induction biases, while RNNs lack spatial sensitivity and require more computation, leading to performance limitations. Subsequent research has focused on network-based and input-based improvements.
1. Hybrid Approaches to Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (CRNNs)
1.1 Some researchers have proposed hybrid algorithms that combine Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (CRNNs) with CNNs and RNNs, achieving better performance than a single network structure. The Transformer architecture has had a transformative impact on computer vision (CV),[20] [21], and natural language processing (NLP),[22] [23], capturing global features through parallel processing of input sequences and attention mechanisms. In addition to CNNs and RNNs, many researchers have recently planned to apply Transformers to AMR tasks. Cai et al.[24] reconstructed and reshaped the I/Q signal sequence into a two-dimensional matrix as the input for the Transformer encoder. MCformer [25] used 2D convolutional embeddings of the raw I/Q signals, then extracted global features through the Transformer encoder. FEA-T [26] introduced a dual-branch gating linear unit (DB-GLU) to fully activate the representation learning of Transformer while reducing model size. Kong et al.[27] proposed a semi-supervised contrastive learning framework based on Transformer, integrating convolutional embeddings into the encoder. Moreover, attention mechanisms are increasingly incorporated into networks, even outside of the Transformer architecture [28]-[31].
In summary, although these frameworks can extract variable features, they still struggle to simultaneously address local feature extraction, temporal dependency, and global feature modeling. While CRNNs help alleviate local generalization biases, they still lack effective global feature extraction. Although the attention mechanism in Transformers can capture global key features, it is less effective than CNNs in local feature extraction and lacks the strong temporal dependency of RNNs. Therefore, there is a need for an effective architecture that integrates the advantages of different neural networks to improve overall performance.
Researchers have employed various pre-processing modalities to attempt the AMR task, such as STFT [32], [33], Markovian transform field [34], eye diagrams [35], constellation diagrams [36], Gramian angle fields [37], and SPWVD [38]. Some researchers have also begun experimenting with feature fusion across multiple modalities to gain richer information [39]. For instance, Qi et al. [40] proposed an HKDD framework that combines instantaneous features, statistical features, and spectral features with I/Q signals, achieving outstanding performance in classification and few-shot scenarios. STTMC [41] extracts spatiotemporal mixed features from I/Q sequences and cumulative active constellation diagrams (APCD), mapping them onto graph structures. Wang et al. [42] used cyclic spectrograms and constellation diagrams, with a dual-branch CNN, significantly improving classification accuracy for each individual modality. Zhuang et al. [43] combined time information with continuous wavelet transforms based on complex Morlet wavelets to enhance robustness against interference.
Overall, the above multimodal fusion methods effectively enrich the input features, but they still face limitations in low signal-to-noise ratio environments. This is mainly because current mainstream backbone fusion methods use separate feature extractors to extract features from each modality, then fuse them through fully connected layers to minimize inter-modal interference. However, in low SNR environments, the effective information of each modality is almost obscured by noise, necessitating maximizing joint information exploration across modalities.
This paper proposes an embedding and fusion framework for multimodal input, IQFormer-based DL-AMR. For time-frequency domain features, Short Time Fourier Transform (STFT) achieves a good balance between resolution in the spatial domain and frequency domain, and its sliding window mechanism on the time axis is very similar to one-dimensional convolution used for extracting spatial features from I/Q signals. Therefore, we choose STFT matrices and I/Q sequences as the input modalities. Next, we employ CRNN as the embedding and fusion tool for the input modalities. Specifically, to enhance cross-modal interaction, we develop a dynamic fusion embedding (DFE) module. This module uses 1D and 2D convolutions to embed multimodal inputs and generate corresponding modality tokens. DFE aligns time-varying trends to traverse and fuse the embedded dimensions of tokens' temporal-spatial and temporal-frequency (T-F) features. Subsequently, we use a Bi-LSTM [44] model to dynamically integrate forward and backward temporal relationships into tokens, constructing high-quality representations that fully leverage the latent connections between multimodal features. Additionally, we propose an effective phase-based block based Transformer architecture. Each block represents a phase, and is composed of multiple Convolutional encoders for extracting token spatial features and an IQFormer encoder based on additional attention for mining token contextual connections along the composition. Thus, each phase learns to combine joint harvesters of local and global features at different scales, allowing the network to fully capture rich semantic representations. The contributions of this study can be summarized as follows.
· Proposed a multimodal hybrid network framework based on I/Q signal and T-F distribution matrix input, and verified the effectiveness of multimodal input in recognizing modulation types.
· Designed a dynamic fusion embedding module that fuses different modality features effectively, jointly harvesting bimodal features on the time trend.
· A Stage-wise Transformer structure is proposed, incorporating additional attention into the model to effectively extract local and global features of multi-scale fused tokens.
· Experimental results show that the proposed method has superior recognition probability compared to existing DL-AMR methods.
Article illustration
Conclusion: This paper applies the hybrid framework IQFormer to the AMR task, proposing a dual-modality fusion framework based on the hybrid framework IQFormer. To address the issue of effective fusion of information from different modalities, a dynamic fusion embedding module is introduced in IQFormer for feature fusion. Additionally, an efficient Transformer encoder is proposed to realize local-global feature extraction. Experimental results on public datasets demonstrate that the IQFormer method proposed in this paper achieves the best performance compared to SOTA DL-AMR methods. The recognition performance of the model shows the great potential of introducing richer feature information through multimodal fusion. In the future, more effective fusion methods and feature extraction methods will be further studied.
